The executive candidate experience is broken

March 2026 · 2 min read

Nobody talks about how bad the candidate experience is at the executive level.

I will.

A couple of years ago, I went through 9 hours of interviews for a CRO role. That doesn’t include the nights and weekends I spent prepping instead of being with my family.

The search was run by a firm you would all recognize. The kind of firm companies pay six figures to make sure they get the hire “right.”

My feedback was strong. I was told I was one of two finalists.

I found out I didn’t get the job from a LinkedIn post announcing the new hire. When I followed up with the CEO, she essentially said it had slipped her mind.

Nine hours of interviews, a retained search firm, and nobody could pick up the phone?

I’ve talked to dozens of senior revenue leaders who have some version of this story and many of us say the same thing. We all felt like a number. Weeks of interviews, multiple rounds, family time sacrificed and then ghosting after being told they were a top candidate. Even when there’s a search firm involved whose entire job is to manage the process.

We hold candidates to an incredibly high bar during the hiring process. They have to show up overly prepared, be thoughtful, demonstrate leadership, send the right follow-up. Then the company ghosts them at the end like none of that investment mattered.

I’ve spent 20 years joining and building revenue teams through sourcing, hiring, onboarding and training, and I know what great looks like on both sides of this process. The companies that get executive hiring right don’t just find the right person. They treat every candidate in the process with the same respect they would expect for themselves, and they actually spend the time to set the person they hire up to succeed once they start.

The ones that don’t? Revenue leaders at these companies talk. The best candidates already know which companies and which search firms to avoid before they ever even consider taking a first call.

What’s your interview horror story? I know you have one.
