When forecasting gets harder, you’re scaling

February 2026 · 2 min read

Forecasting getting harder is a good sign! It means you’re scaling.

When the team is small, you can rely on your “gut” call because information flows easily. Once the team grows, small inconsistencies add up and you end up explaining the number over and over again. Often the challenge is simple: people are using the same words to talk about numbers, but with different meanings.

If you’re in the early-ish stages of revenue growth, stop the inconsistencies before they begin.

DO:

  • Build realistic stage definitions for this exact stage of growth.
  • Create clear entry and exit criteria for each stage.
  • Define simple (simple!) CRM fields that reinforce your stage language. The goal here is to make skipping steps hard.
  • Assign one owner to stage hygiene and deal progression.
  • Implement a weekly cadence with one question: what changed since last week and why?

DO NOT:

  • Try to cram everything into the revenue meeting (forecast, deal strategy, pipeline generation, enablement). Meetings will run too long and you won’t have clear objectives, outcomes or owners.
  • Over-instrument your CRM. Too many stages, for example, will give you useless data and create resentment among your sales team. Capture and discuss only what actually changes decisions (e.g. champion / blocker, next meeting date, close criteria).
  • Build on a culture of fear. If your team is afraid to tell you they’re going to lose a deal, you’ll incentivize lying and ultimately ingest incorrect data. And your culture will go downhill, fast. I have a lot more to say on this topic, but I’ll save it for another post.
  • Try to make every definition fit every motion. SMB / Enterprise, new logo / renewal, etc.

Once you decide and commit to your system and language, keep it consistent for at least 3 quarters and then test, learn and iterate. If you make too many changes to it in a short time, your data will not be accurate, your team will be frustrated and your culture will suffer.

What’s frustrating you about forecasting setting right now? Stage definitions, CRM hygiene, leadership direction or something else?
