Deciding you actually want to know

April 2026 · 1 min read

The hardest part of fixing your revenue org isn’t figuring out what’s broken.

It’s deciding you actually want to know.

Most founders say they want honest feedback from their team, but saying it and meaning it are two very different things. The truth usually means more work, harder conversations and admitting that something you built isn’t working the way you thought it was over a period of time.

The team picks up on that vibe, consciously or not, and they start telling you what feels safe instead of what’s actually real. They agree on what they’re going to say before they walk into the room and the real conversation stays at the lunch table.

I worked with a team that knew their narrative wasn’t clicking with clients but nobody was willing to say it out loud. When we got people talking anonymously, with no names attached to anything, the truth came out fast. We quickly rewrote the narrative and they saw a 21% lift in confidence around it within the quarter.

That’s why I built CARRY1 Compass. Your team will tell an anonymous AI things they will never tell you, and that’s not a reflection on you as a leader. It’s just how people think and work.

The first step is deciding you actually want to know.
