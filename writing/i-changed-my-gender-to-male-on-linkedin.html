I changed my gender to male on LinkedIn

April 2026 · 2 min read

I changed my gender to male on LinkedIn last month. I hate that it worked.

I’ve never been the most technical person in the room, and I’ve always known that, which meant I had to figure out how to be effective in a different way. I learned how to read a room, how to say hard things in a way people could hear, and when to push and to hold back. I told leadership teams things they didn’t want to hear, and I admitted when I didn’t know something to both my clients and direct reports, which in sales is not always how you gain credibility. For almost 20 years, the way I operated was to be exactly who I am, and I know it made me better at the job, especially as a leader, and even when it made other things harder.

When I changed that gender setting and watched my next post crush my benchmarked metrics, I felt guilty. The whole reason authenticity matters to me as a leader isn’t just philosophical, it’s practical. When people feel safe enough to show up as exactly who they are, they contribute more, they trust each other more, they solve their clients’ real problems, and the business is much stronger for it. That only works if it starts at the top, and it only starts at the top if it’s real.

I had a conversation recently with a founder at an ad tech company who told me upfront that working together was going to be rigorous, that they’d call me nights, weekends, and vacations. I’m excited to work with them, not only because I think the product is powerful, their future is very bright and the people are smart, but because they were honest with me from the start.

I’m not changing my gender back, despite thinking about it a lot. If LinkedIn is going to discriminate against my message because I’m a woman, then I’ll game the algorithm to make sure my voice still gets out there. I’ve gotten my mind around the fact that the gender marker is a technicality but this voice is still mine and I don’t want them suppressing it.

If you’re wondering why I selected ‘prefer not to say’ for everything else once a platform shows you how it uses your identity, you stop volunteering more of it.

I’d love to see a man change his gender to female because he thinks it’ll give him a leg up. Nobody’s running that experiment, are they? I think we all know why.
