Being right about who you are isn’t enough

March 2026 · 1 min read

Being right about your identity doesn’t mean you’ll be able to sell it.

When I was at Reddit, our leadership team made a call: we’re not social. We’re not digital. We’re community!

They were right. Reddit was community in a way no other platform could really own. That didn’t mean it was going to work.

When my team went into agencies, nobody knew what to do with us. They didn’t have “community” people. Their teams were split into digital and social. There was no budget line for what we were selling. We couldn’t even get meetings because the people who should have been buying didn’t know they were supposed to be the buyers.

So, we did two things at once. On the ground, my team figured out how to translate what Reddit actually was into language those buyers could understand. At the same time, we brought real feedback to leadership about what was and wasn’t landing in market.

It took the one thing nobody wants to hear these days: time.

And even today Reddit still doesn’t sit neatly in either bucket. That ended up being a strength, but it sure didn’t feel like one when we couldn’t get a meeting.

The biggest thing I took from that experience is that it doesn’t matter how right you are about who you are if your buyer doesn’t have a way to buy it yet. Your team will tell you when there’s a gap, so make sure you’re listening to them. They’re your best signal.
