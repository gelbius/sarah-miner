Your first meeting is for listening

June 2026 · 1 min read

The average sales rep talks 72% of their first meeting. All the while, their client is praying for a fire drill.

AI was supposed to make sellers smarter, but mostly it’s just given them more material to hide behind. Every sales person walks in over-researched with a perfect deck, ready to talk at you for 45 minutes.

Yes, research your client before you walk in the door and go in with a real point of view, but be willing to let that point of view change the second you sit down. Your first meeting is about listening. Active listening. It’s about understanding your client’s point of view and key objectives, not just the company’s priorities, but what that individual is personally trying to accomplish this year. Those two things are rarely the same, and most reps never think to ask.

Just make sure the conversation goes both ways and that when you do speak, you’re adding something real without taking the meeting back over. You need to come in educated enough on their business and what you have to offer that you can show them something they haven’t thought about yet.

Pick one or two things you can help with and bring them into the conversation where they fit. Then go back after that meeting and build something around what you actually heard.

Top-performing reps pitch their offer only 7% of the time in a first meeting.

The other 93% is curiosity and letting them know you came there to understand them.

Your deck is a security blanket. It keeps you in control and protects you from the discomfort of a conversation you can’t see coming. But that discomfort is where trust actually comes from, and trust is what closes deals.

What would change if your team walked in thinking they were there to learn, not teach?
