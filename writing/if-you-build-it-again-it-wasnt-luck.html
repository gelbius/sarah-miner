If you build it again, it wasn’t luck

April 2026 · 1 min read

The week I came back from having Jack, I almost quit Reddit.

My team had gone from 130% of goal to 30% while I was out. Most of my people had moved to other teams. My top accounts too. I sat with what was left and seriously thought about walking away.

My husband, Andrew, said one thing that stopped me. He said: if you build it again, it wasn’t luck.

So I listened first. For days, everyone who needed to talk got to talk. Then I put a plan together and told the team it was time to move forward.

One of my favorite sellers was furious about what had happened. I was pretty sure he was going to leave. We had a real conversation and he stayed. I’m going to his wedding this summer.

By 2021 we were back at 130%+ growth. New clients. New team. People who are still at Reddit today.

At that point I knew it wasn’t luck. So I kept building. New teams, new challenges.

And now I’m building CARRY1. Some days it’s the hardest thing I’ve ever done. But I’ve thought that before.

Jack turns seven today. I’m really glad I stayed.
