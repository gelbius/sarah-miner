The onboarding plan you don’t have

April 2026 · 2 min read

Your lack of an onboarding plan is why you won’t hit your Q2 goal, and not because of the reason you think.

It’s not about your new sellers ramping as quickly as possible. It’s about what’s happening to the rest of your team while they do.

If you’re a seller carrying a number right now and a new hire just started on your team, you already know what’s coming. Your Monday morning outreach block turns into an hour of answering questions about where to find the rate card or how to pull custom reporting. Your pipeline review prep gets pushed because your new buddy needs help figuring out who to talk to about CRM login issues. You’re not doing your job anymore, you’re doing two jobs, and nobody adjusted your quota to account for it.

I asked a sales team recently what their onboarding process looked like and the room got quiet. Some people laughed a bit. One of the senior AEs finally said, “you’re looking at it.”

This is happening at almost every early stage growth company I work with right now. The most tenured people on the team, the ones carrying the biggest quotas, end up becoming the unofficial onboarding program, and it leads to burnout. It doesn’t play out dramatically, like someone quitting. It’s quieter. Your best seller stops doing the extra prospecting, stops showing up early, starts doing just enough to get by because they’re exhausted from carrying someone else’s ramp on top of their typical workload. Obviously, their attainment takes a hit.

The fix isn’t complicated, but it does require you, as a leader, to take a beat from your day-to-day. Write down an ideal plan. Build a 30-60-90 by role with clear milestones, not just activities. Give the manager a guide for what they own in weeks 1, 2, 4, and 8. Put everything in one place so nobody has to ask a teammate “where can I find case studies?”

Your team already knows what new hires need to learn. Someone just has to capture it and formalize it. That someone is you.
