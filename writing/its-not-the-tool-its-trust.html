It’s not the tool, it’s trust

March 2026 · 1 min read

People felt like they were being watched.

That’s what happened when we rolled out Gong at Reddit, and it didn’t matter that leadership said it was about coaching and improving because the team heard surveillance, just another way to track them and potentially manage them out later.

We got past it eventually, but it took work. It took time and actual conversations about what the tool was for and what it wasn’t and it really only worked because the team already trusted the people having those conversations with them.

I think about that a lot right now watching companies roll out AI tools across their sales teams. The technology might be different but the fear is exactly the same, and if your team isn’t adopting the tools you bought the problem probably isn’t the tool, it’s most likely trust. People don’t resist new tools because they’re lazy, they resist because nobody told them why it won’t be used against them, and right now that’s harder than ever because your team isn’t just wondering if the tool is watching them, they’re wondering if it’s replacing them.

This is one of the first things I look at when I work with revenue teams, not which tools you bought but whether your people actually trust the AI system they’re being asked to use… and the real humans rolling them out.
