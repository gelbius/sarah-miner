The year we rang the bell

March 2026 · 2 min read

My husband took a year off work so I could be at the New York Stock Exchange when Reddit went public.

I think about that a lot now because I’m sitting in my mom’s childhood bedroom in Connecticut trying to build a business and it’s easy to forget how we got here. The year before the IPO was probably the hardest stretch of our marriage and the hardest stretch of my career. I was overseeing the media & entertainment business and the Hollywood strikes hit. Our sales team was under major pressure, month after month. I was remodeling forecasts constantly, moving people off my team to verticals that were growing faster and trying make sure my team’s numbers stayed healthy enough that nobody lost their job. I was on a flight to LA at least once a month and when I’d get home, Andrew would be exhausted because he’d been up until 3am at his big law firm trying to hit his billable hours with absolutely no support professionally and two kids under five in the house.

He was regularly running on 3 hours of sleep and we were so tired we could barely talk to each other. Then, Andrew left big law and took a full year off so that I could be there when we rang the bell.

Andrew’s at a tech company now that actually understands he’s a person. He takes Jack and Teddy to sports practices and I sit in this bedroom and figure out what CARRY1 is going to be, which some days feels exciting and some days feels like I’m just talking to myself for eight hours on the verge of becoming Jack Torrance.

I had this dream of building something of my own and now I’m living it and it’s a lot harder than I imagined, but it’s mine. I wouldn’t be here if Andrew and I hadn’t survived that year. You find out a lot about your partner when everything is really hard. We didn’t know it would all work out at the time, but we just kept showing up for each other until it did. I think that’s how most hard things work.

When I was in labor or running the NYC Marathon or during this specific time in my career, I kept coming back to the same thought. Just show up. Keep going. It’s not romantic or especially profound, but it works, and eventually you come out on the other side (it’s true, you do come out!) and you can finally see what it was all for.
