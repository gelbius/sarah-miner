What’s still working after I’m gone

March 2026 · 1 min read

Most consultants charge you to tell you what you already know, right?

Here’s how I think about my work differently.

  • I find the real problem, and I do the real work to fix it. Generic consulting frameworks exist because they scale easily, but that’s not what I do. I take the time to get to know the people and figure out what’s actually not working, which is usually not what the leadership team thinks it is. The forecast isn’t off because of your CRM, it’s off because your team doesn’t feel safe telling the truth, and you have to be ready to hear that for anything to actually change.
  • I care and people feel it. I still have calls with the Spotter team despite my engagement there ending at the end of 2025. I’m not billing for those calls. I do them because I’m deeply invested in the people and the outcome, and walking away when the contract ends wouldn’t feel right. My clients’ successes are my successes, no matter if they’re still paying me.
  • I know myself well and I’m honest about what I’m good at and what I’m not. I won’t take work I can’t deliver on, and when I do take it, I’m clear about what you can expect and I hold myself to it. If you hire me, you should see at least a 20% lift in confidence around your company narrative. Let’s agree on what success looks like before we start. And if I’m not the right person for a project, I bet I know someone who is.
  • I leave a lot behind that doesn’t need me. Career ladders your managers can actually use, a narrative your team believes in, coaching principles that work long-term.

The measure of a good engagement isn’t how it felt while I was there. It’s what’s still working after I’m gone.
