Hook the heart

May 2026 · 2 min read

I spent years explaining Reddit to advertisers. It took a breech baby to make me feel it.

I was about 30 weeks pregnant when I found out my son Jack’s head was in my ribs and I was going to need a scheduled c-section. I had joined the April 2019 bumper community on Reddit, which is one of the subreddits where women due in the same month find each other and go through pregnancy together, and when I got my news I went to the community and found dozens of other women learning the same thing that same week. All of us grieving a little, all of us adjusting to a birth that wasn’t going to look the way we’d imagined, and there was this other thing happening too: I felt guilty for being disappointed at all. I was lucky to be pregnant, Jack was healthy, I knew that, but I was still sad and I needed somewhere to talk about that without feeling selfish about it, and those women were the only ones who understood exactly why, because they were sitting with the same feeling at the same time. That’s when I understood that Reddit wasn’t just a place for headphone recommendations, but a place for a woman alone on her couch at 11pm who needs to know she’s not the only one, and who maybe also needs a community.

That changed how I sold. I wasn’t pitching Reddit as a place to find the right headphones anymore (although it’s definitely good for that), I was selling what I’d felt in that moment, and that is a completely different conversation. And, no, it didn’t matter that I wasn’t selling to Pampers at the time.

At Disney we had a saying: hook the heart. Marketers have known this forever. They constantly think about it in everything from font choices to color palettes to the feeling a brand leaves you with when you walk away. Around 70% of buying decisions, including B2B ones, are made emotionally first and rationalized after the fact. We feel, we decide, and then we explain to ourselves why. But somewhere between the brand team and the sales floor, this idea gets lost. We don’t ask sellers enough what the brand they represent means to them. I think you have to find your own version of “hook the heart” before you can ever use it on a buyer. You have to find the thing about what you’re selling that is actually meaningful to you and lead with that, because if you don’t believe it, they won’t either.

I don’t think it’s a coincidence that my revenue doubled after I stopped leading with headphones and started leading with the c-section story.

Find the story that is true for you, and watch what happens when you stop pitching and start believing.
