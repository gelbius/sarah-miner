What happened to the librarians

August 2026 · 2 min read

What do you think happened to librarians in 1998? It has a lot more to do with being a seller than you think.

For a century, the librarian’s job was all about one thing: information was scarce and they were the gatekeepers. Remember card catalogs? Reference desks? Knowing which volume would give you what you need? I can smell it now.

And remember, the card catalog wasn’t the books. It was the hoop you jumped through to get to the books. The library decided how it worked, and you couldn’t skip it.

We do the same thing to buyers. We make them sit through a call just to get a straight answer on pricing. We make them ask us for things they should be able to find on their own.

Then Google came along and made looking things up free, instant, and completely self-serve.

Obviously reference desk visits collapsed and, sadly, the whole profession got written off as obsolete.

But the good librarians didn’t disappear. They rebuilt the job around what Google couldn’t do. Judgment. Which sources can you trust? Is this answer actually right? What question should you have asked instead?

Looking things up became free and accessible, so knowing what to trust became the product.

This is us. Right now.

All that “adding value by educating buyers” is the reference desk. AI does it now, for free, at 2am, and it never asks anyone to book a call first. Buyers show up with the basics already covered and they’ve read the comparisons, priced the options, and probably seen the demos.

Google was founded in September 1998, while the reference desks were still busy, and nobody there thought it was the end of anything. For us, that moment was November 2022.

Yet 83% of people still read a full article to check a purchase after asking AI.

The homework is free now, but feeling sure about a decision isn’t.

The librarians who kept looking things up? They didn’t make it far. The ones who moved further up in the decision-making process, to judgment and trust, became more valuable than ever.

And I really do miss the smell of that place.
